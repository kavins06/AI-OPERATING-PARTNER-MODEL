# Engagement Sequence

## Practical Order

Use `source-grounded-consulting` throughout as the evidence rule.

1. Executive opportunity terrain mapping and `data-strategy-workshop`.
2. Opportunity terrain synthesis and discovery-zone sequencing.
3. `voice-interview-discovery` across organizational layers.
4. Evidence need consolidation and curated planning-evidence follow-up.
5. AI-native workflow specification / `workflow-mapping`.
6. Organizational intelligence diagnostic / `information-ecology-diagnostic`.
7. Controlled validation review and object-driven governance resolution / `data-governance-discovery`.
8. `knowledge-map-capture`.
9. `tacit-to-explicit-knowledge-capture`.
10. Measurement intelligence and analytics readiness / `analytics-maturity-assessment` and `dashboard-kpi-design`.
11. Business value case / `data-monetization-canvas`.
12. Technical implementation blueprint / `warehouse-lakehouse-blueprint`.
13. Risk and control model / `privacy-security-risk-register` and `zero-trust-agent-controls`.
14. `ai-agent-readiness-assessment`.
15. Machine-readable implementation decision packet.
16. Machine-readable managed lifecycle object / `managed-agent-lifecycle`.

## Who Does What

| Work | Human Operating Partner | AI | Client |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opportunity terrain | Set strategic frame | Draft terrain capture | State direction, operating context, leverage areas, constraints, and decision rights |
| Discovery sequencing | Interpret terrain, choose discovery tiers, and set interview/artifact plan | Draft synthesis, zone map, rationale, and plan | Confirm priorities, access, people, and constraints |
| Interviews | Choose roles, monitor quality, and curate later evidence requests | Ask adaptive questions, capture edge cases, capture information objects and source access paths, log silent evidence needs, and score interview quality | Answer with real examples; no live uploads required |
| Evidence follow-up | Consolidate evidence needs; decide what planning evidence to request; set redaction, access scope, and handling rules | Cluster requests, identify duplicates, draft owner-grouped planning-evidence request, and flag future implementation access signals | Provide only approved, scoped, preferably redacted planning evidence or explain unavailability |
| AI workflow spec | Challenge structure, evidence, confidence, variation, source access coverage, and unknowns | Draft structured organizational workflow intelligence object with information objects and source access profiles | Correct the model and later validate it |
| Diagnosis | Interpret context, select scale, and route interventions | Link findings to workflow-spec objects, separate symptoms/root causes, detect patterns, and draft routes | Confirm facts and clarify disputed interpretations |
| Controlled validation and governance resolution | Choose validators and authorized resolvers, generate role-specific and object-specific views, protect sensitive model content, and decide whether to continue | Draft validation views, prefilled information object/source access register, source inventory, governance prompts, and structured validation/governance events | Correct role-relevant slices and make binding access-path, source, owner, sensitivity, retention, access, and permitted-AI-action decisions without receiving the full organizational intelligence object |
| AI knowledge and guideline requirements | Decide which knowledge and future AI guidelines matter enough to capture | Synthesize prior steps into decision-linked knowledge requirements, likely experts, evidence, confidence, examples needed, and Step 9 routes | Confirm only targeted high-value, high-risk, or low-confidence knowledge ownership/gaps |
| AI guidance pack | Choose which requirements deserve codified guidance; challenge vague rules, missing examples, weak source hierarchy, or unsafe behavior | Draft machine-readable guidance spec, Markdown skill-style view, and test/evaluation cases from Step 8 requirements and expert examples | Experts validate rules, tacit cues, examples, review criteria, forbidden behaviors, escalation triggers, and ownership |
| Measurement intelligence and analytics readiness | Decide which metrics matter, challenge vague KPIs, judge materiality of trust gaps, and route unresolved measurement issues | Prefill from Steps 1-9, extract decision-linked metric needs, find candidate sources, detect conflicts, draft KPI definitions, score maturity, and propose trust classifications with evidence | Confirm source authority, formulas, grain, owners, freshness, quality checks, access boundaries, and allowed AI usage through targeted review of planning evidence |
| Business value case | Judge credibility, priority, strategic fit, and whether the opportunity is worth carrying forward | Draft value hypotheses, link them to workflows and trusted/conditional measurements, estimate conservative/base/upside ranges where evidence supports them, and flag weak assumptions | Confirm volumes, costs, business priorities, acceptable assumptions, risk tolerance, and which value matters to leadership |
| Technical implementation blueprint | Decide blueprint depth, challenge overengineering, align future build sequence to value/risk, and keep the work pre-build | Draft systems/sources, access options, preferred/fallback/blocked paths, canonical entities, identity resolution, normalization, runtime/orchestration requirements, hosting/environment requirements, tool/MCP contracts, document/email scope, test strategy, credential/secrets plan, future access package, and build sequence | IT/data/security/system/vendor owners confirm feasibility, hosting constraints, identity/network/security patterns, sandbox/test-data options, audit/logging, and blocked paths; business owners confirm the path supports the workflow and value case |
| Risk and control model | Judge materiality, challenge over/under-control, align controls to value and risk, and route unresolved protocols | Draft risk register, data classification, tool-level permissions, output controls, approval gates, logging, monitoring, incident response, revocation, residual risk, and stop conditions from Steps 5-12 | Risk/security/legal/compliance confirm sensitive data, prohibited actions, retention, external sharing, and approvals; business owners confirm approval thresholds; IT/data owners confirm logging, revocation, service-account, access-scope, and monitoring feasibility |
| Readiness | Challenge the score, choose the minimum safe behavior level, decide whether gaps are blocking, and recommend the Step 15 path | Build the readiness object from Steps 5-13, apply hard gates, score dimensions with evidence and confidence, map dependencies, name blockers, and propose first safe behavior | Confirm only unresolved business priority, source, access, control, owner, and feasibility facts; accept priorities/fixes before any later implementation approval |
| Implementation decision packet | Decide final first-build scope or remediation path; ensure the packet is complete, machine-readable, audience-safe, and still pre-build | Assemble the canonical YAML/JSON implementation packet from Steps 1-14, including runtime pattern, model capability requirements, RAG/retrieval requirements, orchestration needs, hosting/environment patterns, and generated sponsor, business owner, IT/data/security, build team, or remediation-owner views | Confirm whether to approve a separate implementation phase after Step 16, assign owners, or fix gaps first |
| Managed lifecycle | Decide whether the lifecycle model is realistic, assign accountability, confirm governance cadence, and approve the future operating contract | Draft the machine-readable lifecycle object from Steps 1-15, including owners, launch gates, validation, monitoring, feedback, incidents, revocation, updates, expansion, retirement, and remediation cadence | Confirm owners, approval forum, monitoring/review cadence, incident expectations, revocation authority, and future implementation approval conditions |

## Decision Gates

- Gate 1: Opportunity terrain is clear enough to synthesize discovery zones.
- Gate 2: Discovery zones are sequenced and client has confirmed access, people, constraints, and priority.
- Gate 3: Interview evidence needs are consolidated, and any planning-evidence request is justified, minimal, owner-grouped, sensitivity-aware, and does not request build access, credentials, broad live system access, all-email ingestion, or bulk unredacted data.
- Gate 4: Initial AI workflow specification captures known actors, roles, org context, steps, decisions, edge cases, information objects, source access profiles, source trust, approval gates, evidence, confidence, unknowns, and enrichment routes.
- Gate 5: Diagnostic findings are object-linked, evidence-backed, confidence-scored, and routed to enrichment or remediation paths.
- Gate 6: Role-specific validation views have been reviewed by the right representatives, the full organizational intelligence object was not broadly handed over, corrections/disputes are recorded as structured validation events, and source access path, source-of-record, owner/steward, sensitive-field, retention, access, and permitted-AI-action decisions are resolved for relevant objects or explicitly marked as blocking exceptions.
- Gate 7: AI knowledge/guideline requirements and tacit-judgment gaps are decision-linked, evidence-backed, confidence-scored, and routed to Step 9 or other enrichment paths.
- Gate 8: AI guidance packs are structured as canonical machine-readable specs with readable skill views and test cases, and expert/risk validation status is recorded.
- Gate 9: Decision-linked metrics are mapped, trust status is classified as trusted/conditional/disputed/untrusted/unknown with evidence, KPI definition gaps are named, and agent-safe metric usage is defined or routed.
- Gate 10: Business value case is evidence-linked, assumptions are explicit, baseline metrics use trusted or conditionally trusted measurements, and recommendation is carry forward, fix first, deprioritize, or do not pursue.
- Gate 11: Technical implementation blueprint includes access options, preferred/fallback/blocked paths, canonical entities, identity resolution, normalization, runtime/orchestration requirements, hosting/environment requirements, future tool contracts, document/email scope, test strategy, credential/secrets approach, audit/logging, future access package, build sequence, and open blocking decisions without live build access.
- Gate 12: Risk/control model classifies sensitive data, links risks to workflow/system/tool/output objects, defines tool-level permissions, output controls, approval gates, logging, monitoring, incident response, revocation, residual risk, and stop conditions, with blocking protocols owner-confirmed or routed.
- Gate 13: Each candidate opportunity has an implementation-grade readiness object with hard gates, behavior-level readiness, evidence confidence, dependency map, blockers, required fixes, minimum safe first behavior, prohibited behavior levels, and a Step 15 path; implementation remains gated until Step 16 approval.
- Gate 14: Machine-readable implementation decision packet names packet type, final first-build scope or remediation path, audience views, unresolved gaps, owners, runtime pattern, hosting pattern, future access requests, future component contracts, normalization, tests, controls, sequence, and does not start implementation.
- Gate 15: Machine-readable managed lifecycle object is approved for the future phase, including owners, launch gates, validation, rollout, monitoring, incident handling, revocation, guidance/source updates, access review, improvement, expansion, retirement, remediation cadence if applicable, and stop boundary.
