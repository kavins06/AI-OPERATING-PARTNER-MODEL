# Engagement Sequence

## Practical Order

Use `source-grounded-consulting` throughout as the evidence rule.

1. Executive opportunity terrain mapping and `data-strategy-workshop`.
2. Opportunity terrain synthesis and discovery-zone sequencing.
3. `voice-interview-discovery` across organizational layers.
4. Evidence need consolidation and curated planning-evidence follow-up.
5. AI-native workflow specification / `workflow-mapping`.
6. Organizational intelligence diagnostic / `information-ecology-diagnostic`.
7. Controlled validation review and object-driven governance resolution / `data-governance-discovery`.
8. `knowledge-map-capture`.
9. `tacit-to-explicit-knowledge-capture`.
10. Measurement intelligence and analytics readiness / `analytics-maturity-assessment` and `dashboard-kpi-design`.
11. Business value case / `data-monetization-canvas`.
12. Technical implementation blueprint / `warehouse-lakehouse-blueprint`.
13. Risk and control model / `privacy-security-risk-register` and `zero-trust-agent-controls`.
14. `ai-agent-readiness-assessment`.
15. Machine-readable implementation decision packet.
16. Machine-readable managed lifecycle object / `managed-agent-lifecycle`.

## Who Does What

| Work | Human Operating Partner | AI | Client |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opportunity terrain | Set strategic frame | Draft terrain capture | State direction, operating context, leverage areas, constraints, and decision rights |
| Discovery sequencing | Interpret terrain, choose discovery tiers, and set interview/artifact plan | Draft synthesis, zone map, rationale, and plan | Confirm priorities, access, people, and constraints |
| Interviews | Choose roles, monitor quality, and curate later evidence requests | Ask adaptive questions, capture edge cases, capture information objects, source access paths, truth production chains, log silent evidence needs, and score interview quality | Answer with real examples; no live uploads required |
| Evidence follow-up | Consolidate evidence needs; decide what planning evidence to request; set redaction, access scope, and handling rules | Cluster requests, identify duplicates, draft owner-grouped planning-evidence request, and flag future implementation access and truth remediation signals | Provide only approved, scoped, preferably redacted planning evidence or explain unavailability |
| AI workflow spec | Challenge structure, evidence, confidence, variation, source access coverage, truth production coverage, and unknowns | Draft structured organizational workflow intelligence object with information objects, source access profiles, and truth production profiles | Correct the model and later validate it |
| Diagnosis | Interpret context, select scale, and route interventions | Link findings to workflow-spec objects, separate symptoms/root causes, detect patterns, and draft routes | Confirm facts and clarify disputed interpretations |
| Controlled validation and governance resolution | Choose validators and authorized resolvers, generate role-specific and object-specific views, protect sensitive model content, and decide whether to continue | Draft validation views, prefilled information object/source access/truth production registers, source inventory, governance prompts, and structured validation/governance events | Correct role-relevant slices and make binding access-path, official-source, de facto-source, truth-chain, owner, sensitivity, retention, access, and permitted-AI-action decisions without receiving the full organizational intelligence object |
| AI knowledge and guideline requirements | Decide which knowledge and future AI guidelines matter enough to capture | Synthesize prior steps into decision-linked and truth-production-linked knowledge requirements, likely experts, evidence, confidence, examples needed, and Step 9 routes | Confirm only targeted high-value, high-risk, low-confidence, or fragile-truth ownership/gaps |
| AI guidance pack | Choose which requirements deserve codified guidance; challenge vague rules, missing examples, weak source hierarchy, fragile truth handling, or unsafe behavior | Draft machine-readable guidance spec, Markdown skill-style view, and test/evaluation cases from Step 8 requirements and expert examples | Experts validate rules, tacit cues, truth-production handling, examples, review criteria, forbidden behaviors, escalation triggers, and ownership |
| Measurement intelligence and analytics readiness | Decide which metrics matter, challenge vague KPIs, judge materiality of trust/truth gaps, and route unresolved measurement issues | Prefill from Steps 1-9, extract decision-linked metric needs, find candidate sources, detect conflicts, map truth production chains, draft KPI definitions, score maturity, and propose trust classifications with evidence | Confirm official source, de facto trusted source, truth chain, formulas, grain, owners, freshness, reproducibility, auditability, quality checks, access boundaries, and allowed AI usage through targeted review of planning evidence |
| Business value case | Judge credibility, priority, strategic fit, and whether the opportunity is worth carrying forward | Draft value hypotheses, link them to workflows and trusted/conditional measurements, exclude fragile metrics unless the case fixes truth infrastructure, estimate conservative/base/upside ranges where evidence supports them, and flag weak assumptions | Confirm volumes, costs, business priorities, acceptable assumptions, risk tolerance, truth-production caveats, and which value matters to leadership |
| Technical implementation blueprint | Decide blueprint depth, challenge overengineering, align future build sequence to value/risk, and keep the work pre-build | Draft systems/sources, access options, preferred/fallback/blocked paths, canonical entities, identity resolution, normalization, truth remediation, runtime/orchestration requirements, hosting/environment requirements, tool/MCP contracts, document/email scope, test strategy, credential/secrets plan, future access package, and build sequence | IT/data/security/system/vendor owners confirm feasibility, hosting constraints, identity/network/security patterns, sandbox/test-data options, audit/logging, truth-rule extraction/reconciliation feasibility, and blocked paths; business owners confirm the path supports the workflow and value case |
| Risk and control model | Judge materiality, challenge over/under-control, align controls to value and risk, and route unresolved protocols | Draft risk register, data classification, truth-production risk controls, tool-level permissions, output controls, approval gates, logging, monitoring, incident response, revocation, residual risk, and stop conditions from Steps 5-12 | Risk/security/legal/compliance confirm sensitive data, prohibited actions, retention, external sharing, and approvals; business owners confirm approval thresholds; IT/data owners confirm logging, revocation, service-account, access-scope, truth auditability, and monitoring feasibility |
| Readiness | Challenge the score, choose the minimum safe behavior level, decide whether gaps are blocking, and recommend the Step 15 path | Build the readiness object from Steps 5-13, apply hard gates including truth production, score dimensions with evidence and confidence, map dependencies, name blockers, and propose first safe behavior | Confirm only unresolved business priority, source, truth, access, control, owner, and feasibility facts; accept priorities/fixes before any later implementation approval |
| Implementation decision packet | Decide final first-build scope or remediation path; ensure the packet is complete, machine-readable, audience-safe, and still pre-build | Assemble the canonical YAML/JSON implementation packet from Steps 1-14, including truth production readiness, runtime pattern, model capability requirements, RAG/retrieval requirements, orchestration needs, hosting/environment patterns, and generated sponsor, business owner, IT/data/security, build team, or remediation-owner views | Confirm whether to approve a separate implementation phase after Step 16, assign owners, fix truth gaps first, or fix other gaps first |
| Managed lifecycle | Decide whether the lifecycle model is realistic, assign accountability, confirm governance cadence, and approve the future operating contract | Draft the machine-readable lifecycle object from Steps 1-15, including owners, launch gates, validation, monitoring, truth governance, feedback, incidents, revocation, updates, expansion, retirement, and remediation cadence | Confirm owners, approval forum, truth governance cadence, monitoring/review cadence, incident expectations, revocation authority, and future implementation approval conditions |

## Decision Gates

- Gate 1: Opportunity terrain is clear enough to synthesize discovery zones.
- Gate 2: Discovery zones are sequenced and client has confirmed access, people, constraints, and priority.
- Gate 3: Interview evidence needs are consolidated, and any planning-evidence request is justified, minimal, owner-grouped, sensitivity-aware, and does not request build access, credentials, broad live system access, all-email ingestion, or bulk unredacted data.
- Gate 4: Initial AI workflow specification captures known actors, roles, org context, steps, decisions, edge cases, information objects, source access profiles, truth production profiles, source trust, approval gates, evidence, confidence, unknowns, and enrichment routes.
- Gate 5: Diagnostic findings are object-linked, evidence-backed, confidence-scored, and routed to enrichment or remediation paths.
- Gate 6: Role-specific validation views have been reviewed by the right representatives, the full organizational intelligence object was not broadly handed over, corrections/disputes are recorded as structured validation events, and source access path, official source, de facto trusted source, truth production chain, owner/steward, sensitive-field, retention, access, and permitted-AI-action decisions are resolved for relevant objects or explicitly marked as blocking exceptions.
- Gate 7: AI knowledge/guideline requirements, truth-production-linked knowledge, and tacit-judgment gaps are decision-linked, evidence-backed, confidence-scored, and routed to Step 9 or other enrichment paths.
- Gate 8: AI guidance packs are structured as canonical machine-readable specs with readable skill views and test cases, truth-production handling is explicit, and expert/risk validation status is recorded.
- Gate 9: Decision-linked metrics are mapped, trust and truth status are classified with evidence, KPI definition gaps are named, fragile truth is routed, and agent-safe metric usage is defined or routed.
- Gate 10: Business value case is evidence-linked, assumptions are explicit, baseline metrics use trusted or conditionally trusted measurements, fragile truth is excluded unless the value case fixes truth infrastructure, and recommendation is carry forward, fix first, deprioritize, or do not pursue.
- Gate 11: Technical implementation blueprint includes access options, preferred/fallback/blocked paths, canonical entities, identity resolution, normalization, truth remediation, runtime/orchestration requirements, hosting/environment requirements, future tool contracts, document/email scope, test strategy, credential/secrets approach, audit/logging, future access package, build sequence, and open blocking decisions without live build access.
- Gate 12: Risk/control model classifies sensitive data, links risks to workflow/system/truth/tool/output objects, defines tool-level permissions, output controls, approval gates, logging, monitoring, incident response, revocation, residual risk, and stop conditions, with blocking protocols owner-confirmed or routed.
- Gate 13: Each candidate opportunity has an implementation-grade readiness object with hard gates including truth production, behavior-level readiness, evidence confidence, dependency map, blockers, required fixes, minimum safe first behavior, prohibited behavior levels, and a Step 15 path; implementation remains gated until Step 16 approval.
- Gate 14: Machine-readable implementation decision packet names packet type, final first-build scope or remediation path, truth production readiness, audience views, unresolved gaps, owners, runtime pattern, hosting pattern, future access requests, future component contracts, normalization, truth remediation, tests, controls, sequence, and does not start implementation.
- Gate 15: Machine-readable managed lifecycle object is approved for the future phase, including owners, launch gates, validation, rollout, monitoring, truth governance, incident handling, revocation, guidance/source updates, access review, improvement, expansion, retirement, remediation cadence if applicable, and stop boundary.
