## Information management

- Domain: Foundations of information management and organizational intelligence
- Definition: The disciplined handling of information resources so an organization can scan, interpret, decide, learn, and act.
- Why it matters: AI agents are only useful when the underlying information environment has trusted sources, clear meaning, and operating rules.
- Business use: Use it to diagnose where a business firm loses time, trust, or decision quality because information is fragmented.
- Related concepts: Information ecology, Data governance, Knowledge management
- Source references: By Sanchez Tacit vs Explicit Knowledge.pdf (page 17), By Sanchez Tacit vs Explicit Knowledge.pdf (page 2), Department of Defense Zero Trust Reference Architecture.pdf (page 68), The Knowing Organization.pdf (page 34), By Sanchez Tacit vs Explicit Knowledge.pdf (page 1), By Sanchez Tacit vs Explicit Knowledge.pdf (page 18)

## Information ecology

- Domain: Foundations of information management and organizational intelligence
- Definition: A view of information work as a human, organizational, process, and technology ecosystem rather than a technology-only problem.
- Why it matters: It keeps AI-agent projects from becoming narrow software installs disconnected from incentives and workflows.
- Business use: Map people, processes, politics, data flows, tools, and decisions before designing an agent.
- Related concepts: Information behavior, Governance, Operating model
- Source references: Department of Defense Zero Trust Reference Architecture.pdf (page 68), data strategy challenges_2025.docx (paragraph-block 1), The Knowing Organization.pdf (page 71), Information Ecology ch3 - Davenport.pdf (page 1), The Knowing Organization.pdf (page 85), Department of Defense Zero Trust Reference Architecture.pdf (page 91)

## Environmental scanning

- Domain: Foundations of information management and organizational intelligence
- Definition: The systematic collection and interpretation of external signals that affect strategy and operations.
- Why it matters: Business firms depend on market, tenant, lender, regulatory, and competitor signals.
- Business use: Design market-intelligence agents that collect and summarize listings, comps, permits, rent trends, and policy changes.
- Related concepts: Competitive intelligence, Sensemaking, Decision support

## Tacit knowledge

- Domain: Knowledge management, tacit/explicit knowledge, and information sharing
- Definition: Know-how embedded in experience, judgment, context, and practice that is hard to write down completely.
- Why it matters: Many brokerage, leasing, maintenance, and asset-management decisions rely on expert judgment not captured in systems.
- Business use: Interview top performers and convert patterns into playbooks, prompts, checklists, and review workflows.
- Related concepts: Explicit knowledge, Knowledge capture, Expertise mapping
- Source references: The Knowing Organization.pdf (page 23), By Sanchez Tacit vs Explicit Knowledge.pdf (page 6), The Knowing Organization.pdf (page 24), The Knowing Organization.pdf (page 153), The Knowing Organization.pdf (page 156), By Sanchez Tacit vs Explicit Knowledge.pdf (page 12)

## Explicit knowledge

- Domain: Knowledge management, tacit/explicit knowledge, and information sharing
- Definition: Knowledge that can be documented, stored, searched, taught, reused, and governed.
- Why it matters: AI agents need explicit procedures, source policies, and examples to act consistently.
- Business use: Turn leasing SOPs, compliance rules, reporting standards, and objection handling into controlled knowledge assets.
- Related concepts: Tacit knowledge, Knowledge base, SOP
- Source references: The Knowing Organization.pdf (page 24), The Knowing Organization.pdf (page 23), By Sanchez Tacit vs Explicit Knowledge.pdf (page 6), The Knowing Organization.pdf (page 153), The Knowing Organization.pdf (page 156), By Sanchez Tacit vs Explicit Knowledge.pdf (page 12)

## Knowledge map

- Domain: Knowledge management, tacit/explicit knowledge, and information sharing
- Definition: A representation of where critical knowledge lives, who owns it, how it flows, and where gaps exist.
- Why it matters: It reveals which workflows an AI agent can automate safely and which require human expertise capture first.
- Business use: Build a knowledge map before automating maintenance triage, buyer lead routing, underwriting diligence, or investor reporting.
- Related concepts: Expertise map, Process map, Capability gap

## Data strategy

- Domain: Data strategy, governance, CDO role, architecture, and Five Vs
- Definition: A business-aligned plan for what data is needed, how it is governed, how it is used, and how it creates value.
- Why it matters: Without data strategy, agent projects drift into disconnected tools and unreliable outputs.
- Business use: Use a data-strategy workshop to prioritize agent use cases by business value, data readiness, risk, and change effort.
- Related concepts: Data governance, Data architecture, Data monetization
- Source references: The Chief Data Officer in Government_ A CDO Playbook3629.pdf (page 5), How to create a data strategy.pdf (page 2), The Chief Data Officer in Government_ A CDO Playbook3629.pdf (page 4), The Chief Data Officer in Government_ A CDO Playbook3629.pdf (page 3), Department of Defense Zero Trust Reference Architecture.pdf (page 68), Notional DRA.V2_Review.pdf (page 19)

## Data governance

- Domain: Data strategy, governance, CDO role, architecture, and Five Vs
- Definition: Decision rights, accountabilities, policies, standards, and controls for managing data as an asset.
- Why it matters: AI agents require clear ownership of sources, definitions, access, retention, and exception handling.
- Business use: Create stewardship roles for CRM, property-management, lease, accounting, listing, and document data.
- Related concepts: Data quality, Privacy, CDO

## Chief Data Officer

- Domain: Data strategy, governance, CDO role, architecture, and Five Vs

## Data product

## Data protection

- Domain: Privacy, data protection, cybersecurity, zero trust, and regulatory risk
- Definition: Policies, practices, and controls that protect personal or sensitive data across collection, use, sharing, retention, and deletion.
- Why it matters: Business workflows contain tenant, buyer, seller, employee, investor, and financial data.
- Business use: Define privacy requirements before connecting AI agents to CRM, email, documents, accounting, or property systems.

## Zero trust

- Domain: Privacy, data protection, cybersecurity, zero trust, and regulatory risk
- Definition: A security model that assumes no implicit trust and continuously verifies identities, devices, access, and activity.
- Why it matters: AI agents expand access paths into business systems, so least privilege and continuous verification become essential.
- Business use: Design agent permissions around scoped roles, source-specific access, audit logs, approval gates, and revocation.
- Related concepts: Least privilege, Identity, Audit logging
- Source references: Department of Defense Zero Trust Reference Architecture.pdf (page 79), Department of Defense Zero Trust Reference Architecture.pdf (page 91), Department of Defense Zero Trust Reference Architecture.pdf (page 90), Department of Defense Zero Trust Reference Architecture.pdf (page 81), Department of Defense Zero Trust Reference Architecture.pdf (page 32), Department of Defense Zero Trust Reference Architecture.pdf (page 67)

## Cybersecurity framework

- Domain: Privacy, data protection, cybersecurity, zero trust, and regulatory risk
- Definition: A structured way to identify, protect, detect, respond, and recover from cybersecurity risks.
- Why it matters: Client-facing AI systems need operational resilience and incident readiness.
- Business use: Use framework categories to scope agent security requirements, monitoring, vendor risk, and response procedures.

## Information sharing failure

- Domain: Knowledge management, tacit/explicit knowledge, and information sharing
- Definition: A breakdown in moving relevant information to the people or systems that need it at the right time and context.
- Why it matters: Many business service failures are not knowledge absence; they are routing, trust, incentive, or context failures.
- Business use: Design agents that route the right maintenance, leasing, compliance, or diligence context to the right reviewer.
- Related concepts: Knowledge management, Incentives, Information flow
- Source references: By Sanchez Tacit vs Explicit Knowledge.pdf (page 17), By Sanchez Tacit vs Explicit Knowledge.pdf (page 2), By Sanchez Tacit vs Explicit Knowledge.pdf (page 1), By Sanchez Tacit vs Explicit Knowledge.pdf (page 18), By Sanchez Tacit vs Explicit Knowledge.pdf (page 8), By Sanchez Tacit vs Explicit Knowledge.pdf (page 19)

## AI-agent readiness

- Domain: Business AI-agent business applications
- Definition: The degree to which a workflow has clear goals, trusted data, documented decisions, safe access, human oversight, and measurable ROI.
- Why it matters: It prevents selling automation into workflows that are not ready for reliable agent behavior.
- Business use: Score each candidate workflow before implementation and choose the first pilot by readiness plus value.
- Related concepts: Data readiness, Governance, Human-in-the-loop
- Source references: Department of Defense Zero Trust Reference Architecture.pdf (page 68), data strategy challenges_2025.docx (paragraph-block 1), Department of Defense Zero Trust Reference Architecture.pdf (page 91), Harnessing the power of unstructured data - EnterpriseTalk.pdf (page 6), Bernard Marr Data Strategy.pdf (page 13), Public Health as a model for cyber info sharing Sedenberg.pdf (page 27)
